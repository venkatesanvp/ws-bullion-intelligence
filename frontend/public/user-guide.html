Bullion Intelligence user guide

Engineering candidate: MVP-06. Owner retesting is pending. This is a private local research tool, not an order-entry system. Training images use generated SYNTHETIC TEST data only.

1. Application overview

Research the four existing NSE ETFs: TATAGOLD, TATSILV, GOLDBEES and SILVERBEES. Identify the instrument, source, price basis and observation time before interpreting a chart. A synthetic banner means generated test observations, never an investment result.

Research results describe available observations. They do not establish data licensing, finality, adjustment consistency, historical point-in-time knowledge or predictive reliability. No new forecasting model or trading capability is introduced.

2. Local startup

The owner's running workspace has not been upgraded by MVP-06. Do not restart or replace it to try this candidate: transient prices and their original first-seen evidence can be lost. Arrange a separately authorized handoff with the existing RAM recovery guard before any runtime change. See docs/04-planning/MVP_RELEASE_READINESS.md and the existing startup/recovery instructions.

Engineers use a separate worktree without the owner's .env: run .venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/engineering.py setup, then build and start. The isolated engineering UI is http://127.0.0.1:15183; its database, ports and synthetic configuration are separate from owner UAT. Run the test action for disposable synthetic verification. This is not a command to deploy the owner workspace.

Enter the local application token only in the workspace login. Do not send tokens in messages or screenshots. A page reload locks the workspace and clears in-memory preferences; enter the token again. Provider credentials belong only in backend environment configuration.

3. Navigation

Choose the ETF listing, permitted data source and price basis. Use Research dashboard, History and quality, or ETF comparisons. Inside Research dashboard choose Daily research, Intraday research, Technical analytics or Forecasting Research.

Indicator periods, chart visibility and selected research view survive ordinary tab navigation and ETF changes within the unlocked workspace. Calculated results are refreshed for the newly selected instrument; old ETF results must not appear as current. Locking or reloading starts a new in-memory preference session.

The Market Snapshot and reconciliation landmark stays accessible. In Technical analytics and Forecasting Research, open its optional summary to inspect quotes. While it is closed it does not poll quotes. An invalid listing or restricted source produces guidance rather than an apparently empty page.

4. Research Dashboard

Daily research uses completed daily observations. Choose optional From and Through dates, edit indicator periods, then Apply research settings. Changes are drafts until applied; the exact table labels the periods used in the last calculation. Use Reset to Defaults, then Apply, to recalculate with conventional defaults.

Use the native Lightweight chart first. Chart engine offers the Plotly fallback. Indicator checkboxes control chart visibility only; exact calculated values remain in the table. Tables initially show 15 rows; use Show more for the next 15. Inspect source quality and provenance for evidence, gaps and limitations.

No history means this source/range has no eligible observations, not a zero price. Existing permitted retrieval may run when Apply requests missing provider history; it remains subject to the existing provider policy, entitlement and budget. MVP-06 adds no provider routes or standing network authorization.

5. Market Snapshot

Quotes and historical closes serve different purposes. A quote time is not automatically a final daily closing observation. Review freshness, exchange-local time and source labels. Reconciliation controls remain subject to their existing qualification and access rules. A snapshot error is isolated to that section; other dashboard navigation remains available.

6. History and Quality

Inspect the selected ETF's observed dates, source evidence, missing sessions, duplicates, adjustment status and known breaks. Never fill a missing market observation with an invented price. A downloaded historical series is not proof that it was available at an earlier forecast cutoff.

Switching ETF on this page and returning to Research dashboard retains indicator settings. A new source or range can legitimately have insufficient history; the catalogue explains prerequisites instead of disappearing.

7. Technical indicators

Defaults are SMA 20, EMA 20, Wilder RSI 14, MACD fast 12 / slow 26 / signal 9, ATR 14 and prior-window Donchian 20. These are conventional descriptive settings, not optimized trading parameters. Existing formulas are unchanged.

SMA averages closes; it needs n eligible observations. EMA weights recent closes and needs n observations for its seed. Wilder RSI compares recent gains and losses on a 0 to 100 scale; it needs n + 1 closes, and a flat gain/loss window remains unavailable.

MACD compares fast and slow EMAs. A complete signal/histogram needs slow + signal - 1 observations (34 at defaults); the MACD line can appear earlier. ATR measures true range in price units and needs n + 1 eligible OHLC observations. Donchian shows the prior n observations' high/low channel, excluding the current bar; it needs n + 1 OHLC observations.

All six are discoverable even when unavailable. A disabled box indicates unavailable values, missing OHLC, warm-up or a gap. A known gap restarts warm-up. Use positive integer periods; RSI must be at least 2, MACD fast must be below slow, and required warm-up must fit the 10,000-bar contract. Forming intraday and current/future daily records are excluded from completed-bar calculations.

8. ETF Comparison

Select one Gold and one Silver ETF, or another compatible pair. Two to ten distinct listings are supported. Choose explicit sources and compatible price bases. Use available common period inspects cached dates only; it neither fills gaps nor downloads history. Check the resulting dates, then Compare prices.

The server uses the intersection of eligible observed dates. Each line starts at 100 on the first common date: index = 100 x close / starting close. The percentage change uses the same start and end for every ETF: 100 x (ending close / starting close - 1).

Historical volatility is the sample standard deviation of simple one-session returns, multiplied by 100. Only adjacent common dates that are consecutive sessions in both reviewed calendars are included. At least two matched returns are required. No annualization is applied. Unreviewed or gapped intervals are excluded and counted; a return across several sessions is not mislabeled as a daily return.

Maximum observed drawdown is the largest percentage decline from an earlier running peak among common observed closing prices. It does not include intraday extrema or missing observations. Both ETFs use the same common observation window.

Strict adjustment compatibility remains the default. For the same DhanHQ source and provider-adjusted basis, you may explicitly acknowledge a provisional as-delivered comparison. This does not verify adjustments or upgrade source qualification. Known price discontinuities, conflicting adjustment references, incompatible currencies/bases/classes and other eligibility failures still block comparison. Expand Diagnostic codes for the original technical evidence.

Synthetic worked example: prices 100, 120, 90 and 200, 240, 180 on three consecutive reviewed sessions both rebase to 100, 120, 90. Both have -10% endpoint change, 25% maximum observed drawdown and 31.819805153395% sample one-session volatility (returns +20%, -25%). This illustrates arithmetic only, not actual ETF performance.

Check freshness and source evidence. Matching dates and basis labels do not establish total-return comparability. Distributions, taxes, fees, execution and future performance are not modeled.

9. Forecasting Research

Follow six steps. First choose the ETF and inspect its latest eligible observation. Second choose a supported trading-session horizon. Third select B01 Last-close baseline, B02 Historical drift baseline and/or B03 Historical volatility estimate. Fourth click Generate Forecast. Fifth inspect the result. Sixth optionally open Advanced Research for historical evaluation.

The lookback selects existing observed closes used by the baseline contract. Unsupported horizons or insufficient history produce a visible limitation; they do not create missing data. Changing ETF, horizon, lookback, model selection or applicable assumptions clears an obsolete result. Generate again for the new selection. Selecting no model disables generation with an explanation.

B01 repeats the last eligible close. B02 extends the existing historical drift baseline. B03 estimates volatility and has no directional price endpoint. A price summary identifies the ETF, origin close/date, target trading session, horizon, endpoint, absolute change and percentage change. B03 appears separately as a return-fraction volatility estimate.

The native forecast chart shows observed prices and discrete forecast endpoints. It does not invent intermediate paths or confidence bands. Failed optional saved-run loading does not prevent generation from valid primary context. These baselines are research references, not proven forecasts or instructions to trade.

10. Forecast evaluation

Open 6. Evaluate historical performance - Advanced Research only when needed. Forecast generation and historical evaluation are separate actions. Inspect history/technical/source evidence in its own expandable section.

For real data, STRICT_POINT_IN_TIME remains the default. It may block scoring when publication, finality, revision or historical knowledge evidence is missing. Zero eligible scores is not zero error. CONDITIONAL_HISTORICAL_REPLAY requires explicit acknowledgement of fixed-vintage assumptions. SYNTHETIC applies to generated verification data; it is not evidence of real predictive accuracy. Modes never change silently.

B01/B02 performance is compared on common eligible origins. Read MAE, RMSE, signed bias, evaluation period, horizon, eligible origins, overlapping windows and disjoint evidence counts together. B03 uses separate volatility metrics. Error/regime views and the session registry remain transient. No statistical significance or model superiority follows from a small sample.

11. Data-quality limitations

Source-use, storage, retention and backup rights require applicable evidence. This candidate introduces no persistent real-market storage. Transient results and preferences can be lost on process exit. Recovery receipts do not reconstruct already lost provenance.

Provider-adjustment semantics, timestamps, finality, revision behavior and strict historical point-in-time evidence retain their existing qualification gaps. Matching dates is not a total-return qualification. Forecasts can remain unavailable for a restricted source, incomplete calendar, known break or insufficient lookback.

MVP-05 open container security findings remain tracked in MVP_SECURITY_ASSESSMENT.md. MVP-06 is not security clearance, an owner acceptance decision, a deployment or a release.

12. Troubleshooting

If sections seem missing, check the selected ETF/source, expand the optional Market Snapshot summary and read the visible empty/error state. Retry the affected section or Apply once after a recoverable failure. Other navigation must remain accessible.

If indicators are disabled, inspect minimum samples, missing OHLC and gaps; do not reduce lookbacks merely to conceal a quality failure. If Apply appears ineffective, check validation messages, requested dates, last-applied labels and provider status. Changing visibility does not recalculate formulas.

If comparison is blocked, read the plain-language explanation, inspect common dates/basis and expand Diagnostic codes. A provisional acknowledgement cannot override a known price break. If Generate is disabled, choose a model and wait for context; inspect unavailable horizons and source status. If a request fails, the page must show an error rather than a fabricated forecast.

If the local forecast request limit is reached, wait a minute, then explicitly Reload cached forecast context or retry the action. Editing settings keeps the failure visible; it does not retry automatically. The existing 30-requests-per-minute safeguard remains active.

Do not post credentials, raw provider responses or personal holdings when reporting a defect. Supply build identity, selected symbol, tab, dates, control sequence and sanitized message. Coordinate any restart because in-memory observations can be lost.

13. Frequently asked questions

Is this mock or real data? Read the data-class banner and source evidence. Training screenshots and automated tests here are explicitly synthetic. Existing owner real-data caches have not been read or changed by this package.

Is a rising forecast a buy signal? No. Baselines and historical metrics are descriptive research, with unproven predictive reliability.

Why do exact tables retain hidden indicators? Chart visibility is a display preference; the exact table preserves calculation evidence.

Why can volatility be unavailable when a chart exists? It needs two matched, reviewed one-session returns; plotting common observed closes does not establish those intervals.

Will preferences survive a restart? No. Preferences are kept only in the unlocked browser memory; durable storage is not introduced.
